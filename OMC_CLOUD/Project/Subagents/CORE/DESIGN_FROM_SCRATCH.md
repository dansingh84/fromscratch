## 0. The one principle
A codec survives unlimited generations exactly when its decoded picture is a fixed point: E(D(E(x))) = E(x). That holds when (a) encoder and decoder share ONE reconstruction pipeline, legality included, so the decoded picture is always a picture the codec can describe; and (b) every encoder decision is a function of data the next encoder also has — the reconstruction, the previous decoded frame, the frame index, the configuration — and the first encoder emits what that canonical procedure returns on its own reconstruction. Nothing is recovered, searched for, repaired or undone afterwards. (Consensus of all four experts; our own measurements S5.338–S5.342 confirm the cell-level version.)

## 1. Frame structure — slices are rate and packet units, not transform boundaries
Today each 16-row (8 at 720p) slice is transformed on its own; its bottom edge is mirrored because the rows below have not arrived. That mirror is the root of the seam line (the last row becomes a first difference; when its highpass quantises to zero it copies the row above — duplicate-row fraction 0.53 luma vs 0.03 in the source; seam step 1.82× the interior step at 0.5 bpp; seam_R.txt §4). Every seam fix since (cross-slice term, two-row blend, fills, extra rows, finer last-row quantiser) patched that mirror, and the blend is the smudge (S5.345).
DECISION: the vertical transform runs CONTINUOUSLY down the frame, line-based, with no slice-boundary extension (Chrysafis & Ortega 1999 line-based wavelet schedule — 1999 open literature; line-based wavelet coding is already recorded in DESIGN.md as JPEG 2000-family practice). A slice becomes the unit of rate control, packetisation and loss containment only. Consequences: no seam by construction; no cross-slice term; no display blend; nothing for the next encoder to undo.
Cost to decide: vertical lookahead. Finishing slice k's last rows needs a few rows of slice k+1, which adds those rows to the capture latency (LATENCY.md model: T = slice_h lines + one slice period + 2 lines). With 2 vertical levels (the asymmetric 2V×5H structure already recorded) the lookahead is a handful of rows.
DECISIVE TEST D1: (i) the exact lookahead in rows for 2 and 3 vertical levels, 5/3 and the chosen update form (§2), and the resulting latency per format (720p50 8-row is the tightest: today 0.500 ms codec-only, 0.806 ms with conversion) against both bars; (ii) coding gain vs today at equal bits (a continuous transform should gain, not lose); (iii) seam statistic (row-phase error, duplicate-row fraction) → must equal the interior.

## 2. Transform and legality — legality inside the synthesis
Expert A's mechanism: in an inverse predict step odd = d + P(even), the decoder knows P(even) before odd, so it reconstructs d clamped into [lo − P, hi − P]; the output is legal by construction and the next encoder's forward step returns exactly that d. It needs the synthesis to be ACYCLIC: every clamp interval must depend only on values already final. The symmetric 5/3 update (even = L − U(H[i−1], H[i])) creates a cycle, because an even sample uses the very H values whose intervals depend on it. Expert A therefore removes the update, but our record answers that: predict-only costs ~3 dB (DESIGN3, S5.212) — out.
DESIGN CHOICE: keep an update step but make it ONE-SIDED so the synthesis stays acyclic. H[j]'s interval depends on even[j] and even[j+1]; if the update of even[i] reads only H values whose intervals do not depend on even[i] and are already final in the processing order (e.g. H[i−2], H[i−3] — past-only taps), then the order "even[i] → H[i−1] clamp … " is a DAG in each direction. In the vertical direction this is also naturally causal (it reads only rows above), which shortens the lookahead of §1. The update keeps a low-pass/anti-alias role; whether a one-sided update keeps the coding gain of the symmetric one is unknown.
OPEN POINT for the review (not yet shown): with an update step the low band is NOT raw samples, so across LEVELS the interval of a level-ℓ even sample (even = L − U(H_ℓ)) depends on level-ℓ H values while L is produced by the coarser level's synthesis; the multi-level processing order that keeps every clamp reading only final values (coarse-to-fine vs line-interleaved streaming) must be written out and checked for cycles before D2 is worth running.
DECISIVE TEST D2: coding gain of the one-sided-update integer lifting (best of a small family of past-only taps, horizontal and vertical) vs today's 5/3 + 9/7-M at equal bits on the reference set (per plane, 0.5/1.0/2.0 bpp). Pass = no loss (owner rule); every predictor/update constant in shift-add form.
FALLBACK if D2 fails (stated, not assumed): symmetric transform + the in-cell projection as the DEFINED reconstruction at both ends (expert B; our W/W′/X/Z measurements: in-cell exact on 100 % of coefficients, quality = clip, zero bits); its open limit is dense rail content (projection stalls), which would remain an open problem, never covered by a visible fallback.

## 3. Quantiser and reconstruction
Dead-zone scalar quantiser, power-of-two steps (keep). At a clamped site the first encoder emits the one-step fixed point q* = Q(clamp(R(q))), r* = clamp(R(q*)); the next encoder's Q returns q* (expert A §2.2, proved per coefficient, no neighbour dependence once §2 is acyclic).
Self-identifying reconstruction points (expert A §3.1): r(q, Δ) = sign(q)(|q| + β)Δ with β = m/2ᵏ, m odd, per band → Δ is read from any non-clamped nonzero coefficient by count-trailing-zeros; clamped coefficients (equal to their interval bound) are excluded. This makes the plan READABLE from the picture — no candidate list, no ranking (the plan class of S5.332 had exactly this cause: bands whose evidence the clip destroyed).
DECISIVE TEST D3: quality and bits of moving the reconstruction points to odd-dyadic offsets (in place of today's texture bias) — must be neutral; and the disjointness table for small Δ.

## 4. Canonical decisions (made from the reconstruction, emitted as the canonical rule returns them)
- Budget: nominal per-slice budget from the configuration + the causal bank of ACTUAL bits spent by earlier slices of the same frame, capped (expert B: canonical by induction — at generation 2 the earlier slices are byte-identical, so the bank is identical). Deterministic stuffing to the exact size.
- Plan: one pass — quantise once at the finest candidate step per band; coarser candidates' indices follow by shifts (nested power-of-two lattices); costs from the static tANS code-length tables; a fixed threshold rule picks the cheapest plan that fits, fixed tie-break (expert B; the JPEG XS-shaped decision). The first encoder then applies the plan-READING rule (§3) to its own reconstruction; if a band's Δ is not readable (all-zero or all-clamped), it adopts the canonical tie-break for that band (coarsest step — never more bits) before finer bands.
- Intra/inter per band: identified from signatures of c and c − pred on the reconstruction; fixed order when both are consistent (expert A §3.3).
- Motion vectors: derived from previous decoded frames (keep; canonical already).
- Sequence state: frame 1 intra, bank empty at frame start, refresh phase from the frame index (keep; conventions). Mid-stream start: identical pictures after one refresh cycle, identical bytes once the phase matches (to be stated in the spec).
- Syntax: exactly one representation per reachable decoded picture and state (expert D) — every free choice above has a fixed tie-break.

## 5. Temporal
Prediction from the previous committed (legal) frame, one reference (C2); decoder-derived vectors; rolling intra refresh by frame index (A5). No change to their logic; the refresh must now also be canonical (it is: a slice refreshed at frame t is intra by rule).

## 6. Entropy coding
Static tANS, per band (keep). Its code-length tables double as the rate-control cost LUT.

## 7. Removed, entirely (not switched off — absent from the build)
The generation lock and its candidate walk; the repair engine (gamut_strict); R1 and every search/recovery; the K-round legaliser (if D2 passes); index capping; the cross-slice XSL term; the display seam blend and its un-blend; the pixel-domain clip; every patch switch of the sandbox trees.

## 8. Work and latency per slice (to be confirmed by D1)
Decoder: one inverse pass with one compare-and-clamp per sample. First encoder: one forward pass fused with quantise-and-cost; one closed-loop quantise/reconstruct pass (the reconstruction is needed anyway for prediction); at most two packs. Encoder at a hop: the same, and the canonical rules reproduce the stream. No content-dependent iteration anywhere. Latency: today's model + the §1 lookahead rows.

## 9. Order of decisive tests (each decides a design choice; none is a patch)
D1 continuous vertical transform: lookahead/latency per format, coding gain, seam statistic. D2 one-sided update: coding gain vs today. D3 self-identifying reconstruction points: quality/bits neutrality. Then a clean prototype built from this document alone (new sandbox, not the patched trees), gated on: legality (0 out-of-range samples, no clip present), generation byte-identity at g2, g3 and g10 through both hop types on the full format/depth/chroma/rate set incl. cut24/extrema/graphics/720p, quality and bits vs today, work per slice, and full-frame colour renders for the owner's eye (no seam, no smudge, no step).
